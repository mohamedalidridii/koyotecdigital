Selvane Vision

Kubernetes GPU cost optimisation (MIG, DRA)

Twenty-four GPUs paid for every hour, a quarter of them really busy

Discuss a pilot with us

In short

Every model team had asked for whole GPUs, so the cluster ran 24 of them day and night at 23% activity. We measured what each model really needs, gave the small ones a right-sized slice, packed the rest onto fewer GPUs and let the idle nodes switch off at night.

Client
Selvane Vision
Sector
Computer vision (SaaS)
Size
120 staff, nine AI models in production
  • −55%on the GPU bill
  • 24 → 14GPUs at peak, 6 at night
  • 23% → 52%average GPU activity

The context

Selvane Vision reads documents and video streams for insurers and logistics companies with nine models on Kubernetes: OCR, detection, classification, embeddings and a nightly fine-tuning job. Each team had asked for whole GPUs to be safe. The GPU bill had tripled in a year, while nvidia-smi said the cards were busy.

The challenge

  1. 01

    A pod gets whole GPUs or none: a model that needs 6 GB of memory holds a 40 GB card.

  2. 02

    "GPU utilisation" looked high, but it only says a kernel was running, not how much of the chip was working.

  3. 03

    Night and weekend traffic is a fraction of the day, yet every GPU node stayed on.

  4. 04

    Sharing must never let one model slow down or crash another.

Our approach

We collect two weeks of DCGM metrics per pod (SM activity and memory, not just GPU utilisation), group the models by real need and give each a right-sized share: MIG partitions for production models that need isolation, time-slicing for development and batch. Whole-GPU jobs move to DRA ResourceClaims that select devices by memory and type. The autoscaler drains idle GPU nodes at night, and every change is compared with the bill before and after.

The solution, step by step

  1. 01

    Two weeks of real metrics

    SM activity and memory per pod: the 24 GPUs average 23% activity.

  2. 02

    Models grouped by need

    Nine models, four sizes: six of them need less than 10 GB of GPU memory.

  3. 03

    Right-sized slices

    MIG partitions for production models, time-slicing for development and batch.

  4. 04

    Claims instead of counts

    DRA ResourceClaims select devices by memory and type: pods pack onto 14 GPUs.

  5. 05

    Idle nodes switched off

    At night the autoscaler drains empty GPU nodes: 6 GPUs instead of 24.

Results

  • −55%on the GPU bill, month on month
  • 24 → 14GPUs at peak, 6 at night
  • 23% → 52%average GPU activity (SM active)
  • < 5%change in p95 inference latency

What we deliver

  • Two-week GPU baseline: SM activity and memory per pod (DCGM)
  • Sizing map: the real GPU need of each model
  • MIG and time-slicing configuration (NVIDIA GPU Operator)
  • DRA DeviceClasses and ResourceClaimTemplates for whole-GPU jobs
  • GPU node pools and autoscaler settings, scale-down at night
  • Before and after cost report

Technologies

  • Kubernetes
  • DRA
  • NVIDIA GPU Operator
  • MIG
  • DCGM exporter
  • Prometheus
  • Karpenter

Timeline

6 to 8 weeks

Team

1 platform engineer, 1 ML engineer (part time)
